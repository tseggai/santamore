### Article 2 — What data we collect

We collect only what we need for a specific task:

1. **When you donate:** first name and surname, email address, amount, payment method, campaign, payment reference, your choice of whether your name is shown publicly and the display name, the language for the receipt. For a card payment the processor may ask for a phone number and address for the bank's verification; that data goes to the processor, not to us. For a bank transfer we see the data on the statement (payer's name, IBAN, amount, payment reference). For a monthly donation the processor keeps the card in tokenised form, and we see the last four digits and the expiry date.
2. **When you register for an event:** first name and surname, date of birth, email, phone, distance or activity, shirt size, emergency contact, health notes you give yourself, the signed Participation Waiver with date, time and version, your choice about photos and about showing your name in the results, the entry fee paid. For minor participants: the data of the parent or guardian who registers, the accompanying person and the person collecting the child.
3. **When you open an account or a fundraiser page:** sign-in email, name, phone (optional), language, and what you publish yourself: photo, story, goal, team. The amounts raised on your page are data about you.
4. **When you submit an application for aid:** the applicant's name and contact details, category of need, amount requested, description and attachments (medical documentation, invoices, certificates), statement on other sources, consents. These are often especially sensitive data (health, social status); they are processed under Article 4.
5. **When you volunteer or are part of the team:** name, contact details, roles, attendance at training, hours, for certain roles declarations and certificates under the Child Safeguarding Policy, and a photo if you agree to appear on the team page.
6. **When you write to us:** what you send us and your address.
7. **When you connect a Strava account** (at your choice, for partner perks): account identifier, name, the activity data that Strava sends (type, date, distance, duration) and access tokens. We do not collect GPS tracks or location.
8. **At events:** photos and recordings, under Article 7, and incident records when incidents occur.
9. **Technical data:** server logs (IP address, time and path of the request, browser type) for security and troubleshooting; cookies under the Cookie Policy.
10. **If you are a sponsor, supplier or partner:** business contact details and contracts.

We do not collect data about children from children: all data about a minor participant is given by the parent or guardian.

### Article 6 — The public ledger and your name

We publish every payment in and every payment out in the public ledger; that is the core of our work. The ledger shows the date, the amount, the campaign or the participant's page, the chapter, the payment method, and the display name you chose yourself or the label “Anonymous”. Your email, phone and address are never published. You may choose anonymity when you pay and request it later for any earlier payment.

Beneficiaries are published only in generalised form (for example “a family from Tivat, treatment costs”), never by name, address or diagnosis, except for a story published with separate, revocable consent.

Participant and team pages show the name, photo and story you published yourself and the total amount raised; the results list shows name, bib number, category and time, unless you chose to have your name hidden.

### Article 7 — Photos and recordings

At events we take photos and recordings for the gallery, reports, posts and the event sponsors' materials. When registering you choose whether you agree to be identifiable in published materials; you may change your choice in your account or by message. Without consent we do not publish you identifiably, except in group and panoramic shots in which you are one of many. We remove any published photo in which you recognise yourself within three working days of a request.

Stricter rules from the Child Safeguarding Policy apply to children: publication only with the parent's consent, without a name or data by which the child could be found, and withdrawal at any time.

### Article 11 — Security

If a data breach occurs that may endanger your rights, we will notify you and the competent authority within the statutory period.

### Article 17 — Procedure for a data subject's request

1. A request arrives at the address in Article 9 or with any member of the team, who forwards it the same day to the person responsible for data protection. The date of receipt is recorded.
2. The person responsible for data protection acknowledges receipt within three working days and, where needed, asks for confirmation of identity in the least intrusive way.
3. The data is located in all systems (platform, email, file storage, processors' records) by email, name and payment reference.
4. The reply is sent within the period in Article 9, in an understandable form; for access and portability as an export in a readable format; for erasure with a statement of what has been kept by law and why (for example, the amount in the ledger remains anonymised, payment receipts remain until the accounting period expires).
5. A request for anonymity of an earlier payment is carried out immediately by changing the display, without changing the bookkeeping entry.
6. Every request and its outcome are recorded in the request log (date, type, deadline, outcome), without a copy of the data.

### Article 18 — Data breach procedure

1. Whoever notices or suspects a breach (loss of a device, a message sent to the wrong recipient, unauthorised access, a notification from a processor) immediately informs the person responsible for data protection and the Executive Director.
2. Within 24 hours: stop the breach (revoke access, change passwords, suspend the service), establish which data and how many persons are affected, and record the facts.
3. Within 72 hours of becoming aware: decision by the person responsible for data protection and the Executive Director on notifying the Agency for Personal Data Protection and, for persons in the EU, the competent authority; notification is made when the breach may endanger the rights of the persons concerned. The persons concerned are notified without delay when the risk is high, with a description, the consequences, the measures taken and a contact.
4. All breaches, including those not reported, are entered in the breach log with reasons. The Board is informed of every reported breach at its next meeting.

### Article 19 — Access and safeguards

Access to data is granted by role and to the minimum extent necessary: a public visitor only the public views; a member their own data; staff by role; an administrator everything, with a log. Access is revoked on the same day a function ends. Sensitive fields (donor email, private notes on beneficiaries, processor data) are inaccessible without signing in and are protected by rules at database level, which is verified by automated tests.

Administrator passwords are unique and use two-factor verification where available; secret keys are kept only on the server; exports of data (for example participant lists) are made to the minimum extent, kept briefly and deleted after use; paper lists are destroyed within seven days. The team receives a short briefing on this policy on taking up a function and once a year.

### Article 20 — Accountability and review

The Executive Director is responsible for the application of this policy; the person responsible for data protection keeps the records and procedures and submits an annual report to the Board (data subjects' requests, breaches, new processors, checks). The policy is reviewed at least once a year and on every change of processing or regulations. Until the Board is elected, its powers are exercised by the Assembly.
