# Conflict of Interest Policy

## I. GENERAL PROVISIONS

### Article 1 — Purpose

These rules set out how the association recognises, declares, records and resolves conflicts of interest, so that every decision about money, people and beneficiaries is taken in the interest of the association and can withstand the question: "How do I know this is not going to your friends?"

The rules supplement Article 22 of the Statute (exclusion from voting), Article 39 of the Statute (the Grants Committee) and Article 30 of the Law on Non-Governmental Organisations (use of assets).

### Article 2 — Who is covered

The rules apply to: the founders; members of the Assembly when they decide; members of the Board; members of the Grants Committee; the Executive Director; employees; chapter leads, chapter treasurers and chapter volunteer coordinators; volunteers and external contractors when they negotiate, procure, assess applications or handle money on behalf of the association (together: covered persons).

Accepting a function, employment or engagement means accepting these rules. Covered persons sign the declaration under Article 10 on taking office.

### Article 3 — Definitions

A **conflict of interest** exists when a personal interest of a covered person, or of a person connected with them, influences or may influence how the covered person performs their duty to the association, or when a reasonable observer would conclude so. A conflict may be:

1. **actual:** the interest exists in the decision being taken;
2. **potential:** the interest would arise if the decision were taken in a particular way;
3. **perceived:** there is no interest, but the circumstances look such that the public may suspect one.

All three are declared.

A **personal interest** is any benefit, monetary or otherwise, including employment, a fee, a contract, sponsorship, a gift, a favour, recognition, an advantage for a legal entity in which the covered person holds a stake or an office, and a benefit for a connected person.

A **connected person** is: a spouse or common-law partner; a child, adopted child or stepchild; a parent, adoptive parent or guardian; a brother or sister; a person with whom the covered person lives in the same household; a company, sole trader or organisation in which the covered person or any of the persons listed holds a stake of at least [[PLACEHOLDER: percentage, e.g. 10]] percent, an office in a governing body or a senior management job; a person with whom the covered person has a current business or debt relationship.

## II. PRINCIPLES

### Article 4 — Duties of covered persons

A covered person:

1. decides and acts solely in the interest of the association and its beneficiaries;
2. does not use their function, information obtained in the association, or the association's assets, name or contacts for their own benefit or the benefit of a connected person;
3. declares every conflict of interest as soon as they become aware of it, and at the latest before the discussion of the decision it concerns;
4. takes no part in preparing, discussing or deciding a matter in which they have a conflict of interest, unless these rules provide otherwise;
5. does not accept gifts, hospitality or favours contrary to Part VI of these rules;
6. answers truthfully the questions of a body about the existence of a conflict of interest.

When a covered person is unsure whether a conflict exists, they declare it. Declaring is never held against a covered person; failing to declare is.

## III. TYPICAL SITUATIONS

### Article 5 — Allocation of funds to beneficiaries

A member of the Grants Committee, the Executive Director, a member of the Board and a chapter officer preparing a file take no part in anything concerning the application of a beneficiary who is a connected person, neighbour, colleague, client, employer or a person they themselves proposed, or the application of an organisation in which they hold an office.

A member of the Committee who themselves proposed a beneficiary declares it and recuses themselves, and the Committee states in its reasons who proposed the beneficiary.

A covered person, their connected person and a company in which they hold a stake cannot be beneficiaries of the association's funds (grant criteria, section 3), except for members of the association who hold no function, subject to declaration and recusal of every person who knows them.

A participant who raises funds on the association's platform for a cause whose beneficiary is their connected person must state this on their fundraiser page; the Committee takes it into account when allocating, and even then funds are allocated only according to the criteria.

### Article 6 — Procurement, suppliers and contracts with connected persons

The association does not conclude a contract for the procurement of goods, services or a lease with a covered person or with their connected person, unless all of the following conditions are met:

1. the conflict has been declared in advance and entered in the register;
2. for a procurement above [[PLACEHOLDER: amount]] EUR, at least two comparable quotes from other bidders have been obtained, and the connected person's terms are no less favourable than market terms;
3. the contract has been approved by the Board by a majority of its independent members, without the participation of the covered person, with written reasons why the choice is in the interest of the association;
4. the contract is listed in the annual activity report with the name of the other party, the subject and the amount.

An in-kind gift or a free service from a connected person is permitted and welcome, subject to declaration and a record of its value.

A covered person takes no part in the selection of a supplier in which they have an interest, accepts no commission or favour from a supplier, and does not disclose to bidders the details of other bidders' offers.

### Article 7 — Sponsors and partners

A covered person who is an employee, owner, member of a governing body or regular contractor of a sponsor, partner or prospective sponsor declares this and takes no part in deciding the terms of the sponsorship, the deliverables or the selection of that sponsor; they may make the introduction.

Sponsorship gives neither the sponsor nor its people any influence over who receives funds. Any attempt at such influence is reported to the Board.

### Article 8 — Employment, remuneration and engagement

Part V of these rules applies to employment, fees and remuneration.

A covered person takes no part in a decision on the employment, engagement, promotion, remuneration, appraisal or termination of their connected person, or of themselves.

A connected person of a covered person cannot be employed or engaged for remuneration by the association for a period of [[PLACEHOLDER: number]] years from the registration of the association, without exception. After that period, only through an open call, by a decision of the Board by a majority of its independent members without the participation of the covered person, and with publication in the annual report.

Volunteer work by connected persons is permitted and welcome.

### Article 9 — Outside roles, information and assets

A covered person declares offices in the governing bodies of other non-governmental organisations, companies and political parties, and public offices, that may come into contact with the work of the association.

A member of the Board or of the Grants Committee cannot at the same time hold an office in a political party or be a candidate in an election; the mandate is suspended for the duration of the candidacy.

A covered person does not use data on donors, beneficiaries, sponsors, volunteers and participants for any purpose outside the association, either during the function or after it. The assets, equipment, accounts and name of the association are used only for the work of the association.

## IV. PROCEDURE

### Article 10 — Annual declaration and register of interests

Every member of the Board, every member of the Grants Committee, the Executive Director, employees and chapter officers sign a declaration of interests on taking office and then every year by [[PLACEHOLDER: date, e.g. 31 January]], and update it within 30 days of any change.

The declaration contains: the covered person's employment and business activities; stakes and offices in legal entities; offices in other organisations and political offices; connected persons who are employees, sponsors, suppliers, beneficiaries or applicants of the association, as far as known to the covered person; gifts received above the threshold in Article 15; confirmation that the covered person has read these rules.

The declarations form the register of interests, which is kept by the Executive Director; the Executive Director's declarations are kept by the President of the Board. The register is available to the members of the Board and of the Committee, to the accountant and to the auditor, and to any member of the association on request in the part concerning members of governing bodies. A summary (number of declarations, number of conflicts declared and recusals, contracts with connected persons) is published in the annual report.

Personal data in the register is processed only for the purposes of these rules and kept for [[PLACEHOLDER: number]] years after the function ends.

### Article 11 — Declaration in a specific case

A covered person declares a conflict of interest to the chair of the body, or to the Executive Director, as soon as they receive the papers or learn of the matter, and at the latest at the start of the discussion. The declaration is minuted with a description of the interest.

If another member of the body believes that a covered person has a conflict they have not declared, they raise it before the discussion; the body decides on it without that covered person.

### Article 12 — Recusal

A covered person with a conflict of interest: receives no papers on that matter, except where necessary for the declaration; leaves the room or the call for the duration of the discussion and vote; does not vote; does not discuss the matter with members of the body outside the meeting; does not take part in implementing the decision where that carries new influence.

The minutes record the declaration, the recusal, the times of leaving and returning, and that the decision was taken without the recused person. The recused person does not count towards the quorum on that item. If recusals break the quorum, Article 39 of the Statute or, as the case may be, section 10 of the grant criteria applies.

Exceptionally, the body may decide unanimously that the covered person remains present only to give factual information, after which they leave the meeting.

### Article 13 — Who decides whether a conflict exists

Whether a conflict exists, and the measures to take, are decided:

1. for a member of the Assembly: by the President of the Assembly;
2. for a member of the Board: by the Board without that member;
3. for a member of the Grants Committee: by the Committee without that member; if disputed, by the Board;
4. for the Executive Director: by the President of the Board, and in case of dispute by the Board;
5. for employees, chapter officers, volunteers and contractors: by the Executive Director, and for chapter officers after obtaining the opinion of the chapter lead.

The decision is minuted or entered in the register. The covered person may ask the Board to review a decision under points 1, 3 and 5; the Board's decision is final.

### Article 14 — Conflict of interest discovered after a decision

If it is established after a decision that a person with an undeclared conflict of interest took part in taking it, the body that took the decision decides on it again without that person within 30 days. If the decision has already been implemented, the body establishes whether the association has suffered a loss and applies Article 19.

## V. PAY, REMUNERATION AND ASSETS

### Article 15 — Principle

The assets of the association are used only for achieving its goals, reimbursing justified costs, paying salaries and remuneration to employees for work actually done, and other purposes permitted by Article 30 of the Law on Non-Governmental Organisations. The assets cannot be distributed to the founders, members, members of governing bodies or employees.

Paying a salary for work done is not a distribution of assets. The difference must be obvious and documented. Therefore:

1. **A contract, not a transfer.** Every person who receives remuneration has a written contract, a job description, records of working time or of work delivered, and payment through payroll with taxes and contributions paid. Without exception, not even for the founders.
2. **The payer is never the payee.** Only the independent members of the Board decide on salaries, remuneration and changes to them (Statute, Article 34 paragraph 3). A person who receives remuneration takes no part in the decision on their own remuneration, nor on another's if they are in the same chain of decision.
3. **Published bands.** Pay bands per position are published in the annual report, and the benchmark data on which they were set is entered in the minutes of the Board.
4. **No relatives on the payroll** for the period under Article 8.
5. **A written opinion before the first payment.** Before the first payment of a salary or fee to any covered person, the Board obtains a written opinion from an accountant or lawyer in Montenegro on compliance with the Law on Non-Governmental Organisations and tax regulations, and keeps it in the register.
6. **No awards out of contributions.** Salaries, remuneration and awards are not paid out of contributions and donations collected for beneficiaries (Statute, Article 34 paragraph 2).

Reimbursement of justified costs (travel costs, materials, equipment bought for the association) is paid against a receipt and with approval, under the expenses rules, and is not remuneration for work.

## VI. GIFTS AND HOSPITALITY

### Article 16 — Gifts

A covered person does not accept cash, vouchers or cash equivalents from anyone in connection with the work of the association, in any amount.

A covered person may accept a gift of token value up to [[PLACEHOLDER: amount, e.g. 30]] EUR (promotional items, food and drink at an event, a letter of thanks). A gift of higher value is declined or, where declining is not possible without offence, handed over to the association and entered in the gifts register with the date, the giver, a description and an estimated value; the Executive Director decides on its use.

A gift from a beneficiary, an applicant or a supplier during a procedure is declined regardless of its value.

Several gifts from the same giver in a year are added together.

### Article 17 — Hospitality and travel

Hospitality from a sponsor or partner (a lunch, a ticket, accommodation) is accepted only if it is tied to the association's business, proportionate to the occasion and entered in the gifts register when it exceeds [[PLACEHOLDER: amount]] EUR per occasion. Travel and accommodation paid by a third party are approved in advance by the Executive Director, and for the Executive Director by the President of the Board.

## VII. REPORTING BREACHES AND MEASURES

### Article 18 — Reporting

Anyone, including members, volunteers, donors and beneficiaries, may report a suspected breach of these rules to the President of the Board or, if the report concerns the President, to the chair of the Grants Committee, at the address published on the association's website. A report may be anonymous.

A person who reports a suspicion in good faith must not suffer any consequences for doing so; any measure against them because of the report is itself a breach of these rules.

The recipient of the report checks the allegations within 15 days, obtains a statement from the person the report concerns and submits findings to the Board, which decides without the members the report concerns.

### Article 19 — Measures

For a breach of these rules, in proportion to its seriousness, the following may be imposed: a written warning; a new decision without that person (Article 14); annulment or termination of a contract concluded under a conflict of interest, where possible; a claim for the return of the benefit or for damages; dismissal from the function under the Statute; termination of employment or engagement under the contract and the law; termination of membership (Statute, Article 37).

Deliberately concealing a conflict of interest in connection with an allocation of funds to beneficiaries or with a contract leads to dismissal.

## VIII. PUBLICATION AND FINAL PROVISIONS

### Article 20 — Publication

The association publishes these rules on its website. The annual activity report contains: the number of declarations of interests received; the number of conflicts declared and recusals per body; every contract with a connected person (other party, subject, amount); pay bands; a summary of the gifts register; the number of reports of breaches and their outcome.

### Article 21 — Training and review

Every covered person receives a short induction to these rules on taking office, and members of governing bodies once a year. The Board reviews the rules at least once a year and after every case under Article 18.

The rules enter into force on the day of their adoption by the Board. Until the Board is elected, the powers of the Board under these rules are exercised by the Assembly, and the powers of the President of the Board by the President of the Assembly.
