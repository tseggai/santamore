## PART B: GUIDANCE FOR APPLICANTS

Shown with the form and sent with the acknowledgement.

**Who can ask for help.** Anyone. You do not need a connection or a recommendation. We raise help for families in trouble, children and young people, small local businesses after damage, and local organisations that work with specific people. People from the area where the money was raised have priority, but that is not a condition.

**What we help with.** A specific, documented cost: treatment, medicines and aids; urgent home repairs, heating, basic things after a fire or flood; school, therapies and sport for children; the consequences of an accident or disaster; equipment or an activity of a school, club or institution. We do not help with regular living costs without an event that caused them, with repaying loans and debts, with costs already paid (except urgent ones), with wages, or with anything the state or insurance pays for by law until that possibility has been exhausted.

**How it works.** You send step 1; within three working days we get in touch and fill in step 2 together, with the help of the chapter lead if you wish. We do not refuse an incomplete application; we tell you what is missing. Once the application is complete, the independent Grants Committee decides on it, under the published criteria, within 21 days; in an urgent case within 48 hours. You receive the answer in writing, with reasons if it is refused, and you may ask once for reconsideration with new evidence. Approved help is as a rule paid directly to whoever is owed (the hospital, the retailer, the school), within ten days of the conditions being met.

**What we publish.** We publish every grant in the public ledger, but without your name, address, diagnosis or anything by which someone could recognise you: only "a family from Tivat, medical costs, EUR 1,200". We publish a story or photograph only if you want it yourself, and only once you have approved the text. Your documents stay with the Committee and the people preparing the file, and with nobody else.

**If you change your mind.** You can withdraw the application at any time by a message to the contact you gave.

**Help with filling in the form.** If it is easier for you in person, contact [[PLACEHOLDER: phone or email]]; a member of the team or the chapter lead can come to you, always in pairs.

## PART C: INTAKE AND PROCESSING (INTERNAL)

### C.1 — Intake procedure

1. **Receipt** (the Executive Director or a person they designate; the chapter lead for applications from their area): automatic acknowledgement; contact within three working days; a check of completeness only, without assessing the merits; entry in the register with the date of receipt and the chapter.
2. **Completion**: an invitation to the applicant to fill in step 2, with an offer of help; the list of what is missing is sent in a single message, not in several instalments; a completion deadline of [[PLACEHOLDER: number, e.g. 30]] days, after which the application is closed as incomplete with a notice that it may be resubmitted.
3. **Connection check**: the answer to point 26 and the register of interests are checked before the file goes to the Committee; every connected person is flagged and excluded from preparation and decision-making.
4. **File for the Committee**: the application, the attachments, a short neutral summary (facts, no recommendation), an urgency flag if the applicant or the preparer so assesses, delivered at least five days before the meeting; urgent applications go straight to the chair of the Committee.
5. **Notice**: in writing, within five days of the decision, using the C.2 templates; for an approval, also instructions to the supplier or beneficiary on the method of payment.
6. **Records**: application status (received, in review, approved, declined, withdrawn, closed as incomplete), dates, the reference of the Committee's decision, method of payment; personal data under the retention schedule in the Privacy Policy.
