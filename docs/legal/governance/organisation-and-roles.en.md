# Organisation and roles

## I. PURPOSE

### Article 1 — What this document covers

This document describes how Santamore is organised: its bodies, who elects whom, who decides what, how a decision is reached when the votes are tied, what every role on the team involves, and what membership brings. It follows the Statute and the statute additions and is written for every member to read.

Where this document and the Statute differ, the Statute applies. The numbers of people given for each role are planning targets from the team guide, not limits.

## II. THE BODIES

### Article 2 — The organisation chart

The chart shown with this document reads from the top down. The Assembly, meaning every member, elects and recalls every body. Under it sit the Board, the Grants Committee and the Executive Director. Under the Executive Director sit the team leads, the chapter teams and the staff. Under them sit the volunteer roles: event-day volunteers, fundraisers and club connectors.

Two boxes are marked as paid: the Executive Director and the staff. Everyone else is unpaid. Pay comes only from sponsorship, entry fees, grants and membership fees, never from donations collected for beneficiaries.

### Article 3 — The Assembly

The Assembly is the highest body. It consists of all members, each with one vote, and meets at least once a year. A member who cannot attend may give a written proxy to another member.

The Assembly:

- adopts and amends the Statute, by a two-thirds majority of the members present or represented;
- adopts the annual plan, the budget, the financial report and the work report;
- elects and dismisses the members of the Board and of the Grants Committee, and the Executive Director;
- admits and expels members.

### Article 4 — The Board

The Board governs between two Assemblies. It has five to seven members elected by the Assembly for four years, with an independent majority: people not employed or paid by the association and not close relatives of an employee. The Board elects its own President, and the Executive Director cannot be that President.

The Board:

- sets strategy and prepares the annual plan and budget for the Assembly;
- supervises the budget and reviews the money at least quarterly;
- adopts the staffing act, hires and dismisses staff and sets pay, where only independent members vote on pay;
- adopts and publishes the grant criteria the Grants Committee proposes;
- founds and dissolves chapters, appoints their officers and adopts the Chapter Rules and the internal policies.

The Board never decides which beneficiary receives funds.

### Article 5 — The Grants Committee

The Grants Committee is the only body that decides who receives the money collected for beneficiaries, how much and for what, against criteria published before they are applied. It has three to five members elected by the Assembly on the Board's proposal, for three-year terms with one third of the seats renewed every year.

Founders, the Executive Director and employees cannot sit on it. Its decisions are final; the Board may send a decision back once, with written reasons, only if it breached the published criteria or the Statute.

### Article 6 — The Executive Director

The Executive Director is the person authorised to represent the association. The Director signs contracts, is responsible for the legality of the work, runs events, chapters, partnerships and staff, implements the decisions of the Board and the Grants Committee, and reports to the Assembly and the Board. The Director never decides which beneficiary receives funds.

The first Executive Director is the founder of Santamore, [[PLACEHOLDER: name of the founder elected by the founding assembly]], elected by the founding assembly. Under the amendments proposed in section 6 of the statute additions the term is five years, renewable without limit, and early dismissal is possible only for a reason stated in the Statute, by a two-thirds majority of all members. Until the Assembly adopts those amendments, the Statute as filed applies.

The Executive Director decides every matter that the law, the Statute or a decision of the Assembly does not reserve to another body, within the adopted budget and the spending limits the Board sets.

### Article 7 — Chapters, teams and staff

A chapter is Santamore in one town: an internal team, not a legal person, with a chapter lead, a chapter treasurer and a volunteer coordinator appointed by the Board for two years. The chapter lead runs the town, reports to the Executive Director at least quarterly and cannot sign contracts without the Director's written authorisation.

A team lead owns one function across the year: volunteers, logistics, communications, photography or partnerships. Staff are employed by the Board under the staffing act. Team leads, chapter officers and staff report to the Executive Director.

## III. WHO DECIDES WHAT

### Article 9 — Hierarchy and override

1. The law and the Statute stand above every decision of every body.
2. A decision of the Assembly binds the Board, the Executive Director and everyone on the team.
3. A decision of the Board binds the Executive Director and the team, except where the Statute reserves the matter to the Grants Committee or to the Executive Director.
4. A decision of the Grants Committee on an allocation is final; the Board may return it once with written reasons, and the Committee's second decision stands.
5. A decision of the Executive Director binds the team leads, the chapter teams, the staff and the volunteers.

### Article 10 — When the votes are tied

- In the Assembly, a tied vote is put to a second vote at the same session after further discussion; if it is tied again, the vote of the President of the Assembly decides.
- In the Board, the vote of the President of the Board decides, except on anyone's pay, on a proposal to dismiss the Executive Director or a member of a body, and on the Director's report, where a tie means the proposal is not adopted.
- Where the Board does not decide on an operational matter the Executive Director submitted, because it lacked a quorum at two consecutive sessions or the vote was tied, the Executive Director may act within the adopted budget and reports at the Board's next session.
- In the Grants Committee a decision needs a majority of all members, so a tie means no allocation; the application is re-examined at the next sitting with the additional information the Committee asks for.

These rules take effect when the Assembly adopts the amendments in section 6 of the statute additions. Until then the Statute as filed applies, under which a tied vote means the proposal is not adopted.

## IV. ROLES

### Article 11 — Founders

The founders are the people who signed the founding decision and the Statute. Once the association is registered, a founder is a member with one vote, like every member admitted later. A founder cannot sit on the Grants Committee. Under the proposed amendments a founder's membership can end only by the founder's own withdrawal or by a two-thirds majority of all members.

What the founders carry is the story, the culture and the duty to recruit the first independent Board and Grants Committee. The founder who started Santamore leads it as Executive Director; the other founders take the roles below like anyone else.

### Article 12 — Roles on the team

| Role | Time | What it involves | Paid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Event-day volunteer | 1 day | Course marshal, registration desk, water station, photographer, suit distribution. Briefed the week before. | No |
| Champion fundraiser | About 3 hours in total | A page, three sentences, fifteen people asked. | No |

| Staff | Per contract | Events and chapters, partnerships, communications, in that order of hiring. | Yes, from sponsorship and grants |

### Article 13 — Reporting lines

- The Executive Director reports to the Assembly and to the Board.
- The Grants Committee reports once a year to the Assembly and the Board, and answers to no one for the content of its decisions.
- Team leads, chapter officers and staff report to the Executive Director.
- Event-day volunteers report to the team lead or chapter officer running their function on the day.
- A fundraiser or club connector works with the partnerships or communications team lead.

### Article 14 — How a role is taken up

1. Say it in writing, to the Executive Director, with the hours you can actually give.
2. Agree a written remit: what you own, until when, and who you report to.
3. Deliver one thing well through the next public event; that is how the team learns what each person is good at.
4. A paid role is advertised, benchmarked and approved by the Board. Being here first is an advantage, not an entitlement.

## V. MEMBERSHIP

### Article 15 — Becoming a member

Any person or legal entity who accepts the goals of the association can join on the recommendation of one existing member. The Assembly admits new members by majority vote; the new member signs the membership form and is entered in the register of members.

Membership ends by a written statement of withdrawal, or by a decision of the Assembly for breaching the Statute or harming the association's reputation, after the member has had the opportunity to respond.

### Article 16 — Rights and duties

A member has the right to:

- take part, on an equal footing with every other member, in achieving the goals of the association;
- vote at the Assembly, in person or by proxy;
- elect, and be elected to, every body of the association;
- propose causes for which the association raises funds, and vote on the shortlist of causes;
- be informed fully and in time about the work and activities of the association.

A member is expected to:

- contribute actively to achieving the goals;
- take part in activities according to their interests;
- pay the membership fee, if the Assembly has set one for that year;
- carry out the tasks the Assembly entrusts to them.

### Article 17 — Membership fee

No membership fee is charged for 2026. The founding members decided this on [[PLACEHOLDER: date of the decision]] and the first Assembly records it as a decision under the Statute.

For 2027 the Board proposes to the Assembly, by [[PLACEHOLDER: month of the proposal]], whether a fee is charged and in what amount, together with the list of benefits in Article 18. The Assembly decides every year; it may decide that no fee is charged.

### Article 18 — What membership brings

- One vote at the Assembly, and the right to be elected to every body.
- The right to propose a cause and to vote on the shortlist of causes Santamore raises for that year.
- The annual report and the hand-over of the money raised, in person at the Impact Day.
- First word on volunteer roles and on team and chapter leadership.
- A members' registration window for the association's events before registration opens to the public [[PLACEHOLDER: confirm for 2027]].
- Member communications: the plan for the year, the results of every event, and the invitation to each Assembly.
- A say in the fee itself: the Assembly sets it every year, and may set it at zero.

## VI. FINAL PROVISIONS

### Article 19 — Access to documents

The policies published on the site are public. This document and the member-facing policies are available to every signed-in member in the member area. The rules the Board adopts, the templates and the registration pack are available to the staff in the console. The pay and staffing policy is available only to the Executive Director and the administrators, because it carries pay figures and personal data.

### Article 20 — Changes

The Board keeps this document current. It is reviewed after every Assembly and whenever the Statute, the membership of a body or the staffing plan changes.
