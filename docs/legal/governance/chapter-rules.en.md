# Chapter Rules

## I. GENERAL PROVISIONS

### Article 1 — Subject

These rules govern in more detail the founding, organisation, operation, finances, reporting and dissolution of the association's chapters, in accordance with Articles 40 to 44 of the Statute.

The rules are the same for every chapter. In its decision founding a chapter the Board may set only those particulars that these rules expressly allow.

### Article 2 — Status of a chapter

A chapter is an internal organisational unit of the association for the area of one or more municipalities. A chapter is not a legal person, has no account, seal or property of its own, and cannot conclude contracts or assume obligations.

Everything a chapter does, it does in the name and on behalf of the association. The association answers for obligations arising from a chapter's work, and persons who assumed them without authority answer to the association.

## II. FOUNDING

### Article 3 — Proposal to found a chapter

A proposal to found a chapter is submitted to the Board by at least three members of the association resident in the proposed area. The proposal states:

1. the chapter's area (the municipality or municipalities);
2. the names of the proposed chapter lead, treasurer and volunteer coordinator, with a brief description of their experience;
3. a plan for a first public event within twelve months, with an estimate of costs and sources of funds;
4. a list of the clubs, schools, companies and other partners with whom the proposers are already in contact;
5. a declaration by the proposers that they accept the Statute, these rules, the grant criteria and the Child Safeguarding Policy.

The Board decides on the proposal at its next meeting, after obtaining the Executive Director's opinion.

### Article 4 — Founding decision and the Chapter in a Box

In the founding decision the Board sets the chapter's name (the association's name and the name of the municipality, for example "Santamore Kotor"), its area, the names of the persons under Article 3 point 2, its starting budget and the person from among the association's staff or bodies who is the chapter's standing contact (the national contact).

Within 30 days of the decision the Executive Director delivers the Chapter in a Box to the chapter, containing at least:

1. the event playbook with templates (plan, budget, risk assessment, permit checklist, run sheet, report);
2. the visual identity: logo, colours, templates for posts, posters and T-shirts;
3. forms for the cash log, expense reconciliation and incident reports;
4. access to the association's platform: the chapter page, the chapter's income and expenditure ledger, the cash log tool and the volunteer register;
5. a chapter email address on the association's domain;
6. the confirmation of insurance covering the chapter's events and the conditions under which it applies;
7. these rules, the grant criteria, the Child Safeguarding Policy and the Event Terms.

## III. ORGANISATION

### Article 5 — Chapter officers

A chapter has a chapter lead, a chapter treasurer and a volunteer coordinator. They are appointed by the Board on the proposal of the chapter's members for a term of two years, renewable (Article 41 of the Statute).

These persons must be adult members of the association. The chapter lead and the treasurer cannot be the same person, nor spouses or close relatives. The same persons cannot at the same time be members of the Grants Committee.

If any of these posts falls vacant, the chapter lead proposes a replacement within 30 days, and until the appointment the duties are performed by a person designated by the Executive Director.

### Article 6 — Chapter lead

The chapter lead:

1. organises the chapter's work and represents it towards partners and the public in its area, within the authority given by these rules;
2. prepares the chapter's annual plan and budget and proposes them to the Executive Director;
3. is responsible for every chapter event being prepared and held in accordance with Part IV of these rules;
4. convenes and chairs the meetings of the chapter's members;
5. submits the reports under Part VII of these rules;
6. receives applications for aid from the chapter's area and forwards them into the procedure under the grant criteria, without assessing them and without promising anything to the applicant;
7. informs the Executive Director without delay of every incident, complaint, media enquiry or circumstance that may harm the association's reputation.

The chapter lead cannot conclude contracts, hire staff, receive sponsorship into a personal account or make public statements on the association's policy without the Executive Director's written authorisation.

### Article 7 — Chapter treasurer

The chapter treasurer:

1. keeps the record of all of the chapter's income and expenditure on the association's platform and keeps the supporting documents;
2. is responsible for cash handling in accordance with Article 13 of these rules;
3. checks that every expense has an approval, an invoice in the association's name and cover in the budget;
4. prepares the financial part of the chapter's reports and works with the association's accountant;
5. does not dispose of the association's funds: has no access to the account and makes no payments, other than paying cash from approved petty cash up to the amount in Article 14.

### Article 8 — Volunteer coordinator

The volunteer coordinator keeps the chapter's volunteer register, organises the recruiting, rostering and briefing of volunteers for every event, ensures that every volunteer receives a short safety and child safeguarding briefing before the event, and records volunteer hours for the annual report.

### Article 9 — Chapter members and meetings

Chapter members are members of the association resident in or permanently engaged in the chapter's area who are marked in the register of members as members of that chapter. A member can be a member of only one chapter.

A chapter holds a meeting of its members at least once every three months. Brief minutes of the meeting (date, those present, decisions) are kept on the platform. The meeting before the annual session of the Assembly, at which the Assembly's agenda is considered, is the condition for the chapter lead to be able to represent members by proxy (Article 17d of the Statute).

## IV. EVENTS

### Article 10 — Annual plan

By [[PLACEHOLDER: date, e.g. 31 October]] the chapter lead sends the Executive Director the chapter's plan for the following year: events with indicative dates, the fundraising target, volunteer needs, planned partners and sponsors, and a budget per event.

The Executive Director reconciles the plan with the national calendar and budget and passes it to the Board together with the proposed annual budget of the association. The approved chapter budget is the ceiling on the chapter's costs in that year.

Every chapter organises at least one public event a year (Article 41 of the Statute).

### Article 11 — Event approval

No event is announced or held without the Executive Director's written approval.

The chapter lead notifies the Executive Director of an event at least [[PLACEHOLDER: number, e.g. 60]] days before it is held, and at least [[PLACEHOLDER: number, e.g. 30]] days before delivers the complete file: the event plan, the budget with the source of funds for costs incurred before income, the risk assessment, the list of required permits and the status of each, the safety and medical plan, the name of the person responsible for child safeguarding, the volunteer plan and the communications plan.

The Executive Director approves the event within seven days of receiving the complete file, may approve it with conditions, or refuses it with reasons. An event that involves activity in the water, the participation of children or more than [[PLACEHOLDER: number]] participants is approved by the Executive Director only after obtaining the consent of the national safety lead or the national child safeguarding lead respectively.

### Article 12 — Conditions for holding an event

For every chapter event the following are mandatory:

1. a permit from the competent municipal authority and, where required, from the police and the road or shore authority, obtained before registration opens;
2. liability insurance covering the event, in accordance with the confirmation in the Chapter in a Box;
3. on-site medical cover proportionate to the number of participants and the type of activity;
4. registration of participants exclusively through the association's platform, with acceptance of the Event Terms and the Participation Waiver; for minor participants, the consent of a parent or guardian;
5. a named child safeguarding lead, visibly identified and published on the event page;
6. a participant limit set in the plan and enforced;
7. for events in the water: qualified lifeguards, a safety boat, a safety briefing for all participants and cooperation with a club that carries out such activities professionally; without these, no event in the water is held;
8. photography and filming only with a published notice, and children identifiable only with parental consent;
9. an event report within the deadline in Article 19.

## V. MONEY AND RECORDS

### Article 13 — Basic rules

All funds raised through a chapter's work are funds of the association and are paid into the association's account. The chapter, its officers and volunteers may not hold the association's funds in personal accounts, in private tills or in cash for longer than these rules allow.

Contributions are collected primarily through the association's platform (card, transfer with a reference, fundraiser pages). Cash is accepted only when no other way is possible.

For cash received, a numbered receipt from the association's receipt book is issued. Cash is counted by two persons, one of whom must be the treasurer or the chapter lead, and both sign the count. The amount is entered in the cash log on the platform within 48 hours and deposited into the association's account within five working days, with a reference linking the deposit to the log. Until the association confirms receipt, the amount is shown on the platform as "handed in, pending".

### Article 14 — Expenses

A chapter may incur an expense only within the approved budget and for an approved event. An expense above [[PLACEHOLDER: amount]] EUR per item, or a total exceeding the budget line by more than 10 percent, requires the Executive Director's prior written approval.

The invoice is issued to the association, with the association's PIB. Payments are made by the association from its account. A person who, with approval, paid an expense from their own funds submits a reimbursement claim with the invoice within 30 days; the association reimburses within 15 days of a complete claim.

The treasurer may run petty cash in cash up to [[PLACEHOLDER: amount]] EUR in total, with a receipt for every item and a reconciliation on the platform within seven days.

A chapter does not pay funds to beneficiaries. Every grant is decided by the Grants Committee and paid by the association (grant criteria, section 11).

### Article 15 — Sponsorship and gifts

Sponsorship, donations from companies and in-kind gifts for chapter events are agreed in the association's name. A sponsorship contract is signed by the Executive Director; the chapter lead may negotiate within the national sponsor packages and price list.

Every in-kind gift (equipment, food, services, premises) is recorded on the platform with an estimated value and the giver's name. Obligations owed to a sponsor (logo, mention, a place at the event) are recorded and fulfilled as agreed.

Chapter officers cannot accept personal gifts, fees or favours from sponsors, suppliers or beneficiaries.

### Article 16 — Split of funds raised in the chapter's area

Funds raised in the chapter's area are distributed by the published formula that the association keeps for each chapter on the platform: [[PLACEHOLDER: percentage, the team guide proposes 70]] percent to beneficiaries in the chapter's area, [[PLACEHOLDER: percentage, 20]] percent to national operations and [[PLACEHOLDER: percentage, 10]] percent to the Solidarity Fund for areas with the greatest need and the smallest fundraising capacity.

Voluntary contributions and donations collected for beneficiaries are not reduced by the association's operating costs (Statute, Article 34 paragraph 2).

The allocation of the share intended for beneficiaries in the chapter's area is decided by the Grants Committee under the grant criteria.

### Article 17 — Records

A chapter keeps on the association's platform: a record of income by event and source; a record of expenses with documents; the cash log; the volunteer register with briefing records; meeting minutes; event reports; a record of permits, insurance and incidents.

Paper documents (cash receipts, signed counts, permits) are handed to the association within 30 days of the event. A chapter keeps no parallel records outside the platform.

## VI. NAME, MARK AND PUBLIC VOICE

### Article 18 — Use of the name and mark

A chapter uses the name exclusively in the form set in the founding decision ("Santamore" and the name of the municipality) and the logo, colours and templates from the Chapter in a Box, without alteration. The name and mark are used only for activities approved under these rules.

Social media accounts, email addresses and web pages carrying the chapter's name belong to the association. The association opens them, the Executive Director holds the credentials, and the chapter is given posting access. A person whose function ends loses access the same day.

The chapter lead may make statements to the media about the chapter's events and work. Statements on the association's policy, finances, beneficiaries or incidents are made only by the Executive Director or a person they authorise. Every media enquiry on those subjects is forwarded to the Executive Director the same day.

Sponsor logos are displayed in accordance with the national sponsor packages. A chapter cannot publish material containing a political message, a religious message or content contrary to the association's goals.

## VII. REPORTING AND COOPERATION

### Article 19 — Reports

The chapter lead submits to the Executive Director, through the platform:

1. **an event report** within 14 days of the event: the number of participants and volunteers, funds raised by source, costs, sponsors and in-kind gifts, incidents and complaints, photographs with the consent records, lessons learned;
2. **a quarterly report** by the 15th of the month following the quarter: activities, income and expenditure against budget, cash position and hand-ins, volunteers, partners, plans for the next quarter;
3. **an annual report** by [[PLACEHOLDER: date, e.g. 31 January]]: everything from the quarterly reports for the year, an assessment of how far the plan was met and proposals for the next plan.

A chapter fulfils its obligation under the Statute (Article 41) if in the year it held at least one approved public event and submitted all the reports under this Article. The Executive Director records this for each chapter separately in the report to the Board.

### Article 20 — Working with the Executive Director

The national contact under Article 4 holds a regular monthly call with the chapter lead and is the first person the chapter turns to for questions, materials and approvals.

The Executive Director directs the work of the chapters (Statute, Article 28): gives instructions, reconciles calendars, approves events and expenses, and may order a chapter to stop an activity that is not in accordance with the Statute, these rules, the law or an approval. A stop order is binding immediately, and the chapter may ask the Board to review it at its next meeting.

Chapters cooperate with one another and share experience; the Executive Director gathers the leads of all chapters at least once a year.

### Article 21 — Child safeguarding and incidents

The association's Child Safeguarding Policy applies to all chapter activities: no contact between an adult and a child without others present, all volunteers receive a briefing, children are photographed only with parental consent, and a child safeguarding lead is named for every event.

Every incident (injury, accident in the water, a concern about a child, loss of money, a dispute with a participant or partner, a media event) is reported to the Executive Director and, where it concerns a child, to the national child safeguarding lead, immediately and at the latest within 24 hours, on the form from the Chapter in a Box. Reports to the competent authorities are made by the association.

## VIII. MEASURES AND DISSOLUTION

### Article 22 — Measures

If a chapter or any of its officers acts contrary to the Statute, these rules or an approval, the Executive Director may: issue a written warning; stop an event or activity; temporarily, for up to 60 days, withdraw a chapter officer's authority and designate a replacement. The Executive Director informs the Board of a temporary withdrawal of authority, and the Board at its next meeting decides on the officer's dismissal or on restoring their authority.

The chapter officer has the right to be heard before the Board's decision.

### Article 23 — Dissolution of a chapter

The Board dissolves a chapter in the cases under Article 44 of the Statute. Before a decision to dissolve for inactivity or breach of the rules, the Executive Director warns the chapter in writing and allows at least 60 days to remedy the grounds.

On dissolution, the chapter lead hands over to the association all records, materials, equipment and credentials within 15 days. Funds recorded for the chapter remain earmarked for beneficiaries in its area, and the Grants Committee decides on them.

## IX. TRANSITIONAL AND FINAL PROVISIONS

### Article 24

The chapter for the area of the municipality of Tivat, with the Bay of Kotor as its initial area of activity, is the association's first chapter and is founded by a decision of the Board at its first meeting after the association's registration, without a proposal under Article 3.

These rules take effect on the day of their adoption by the Board and are published to members on the association's platform. The Board reviews them at least once a year, on the Executive Director's report on the work of the chapters.
