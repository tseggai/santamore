# Guidance on drafting the statute of an association

English translation of *Uputstvo za izradu statuta udruženja*. Translation only; editorial notes are marked **[Translator's note]**.

## Statute of an association

In accordance with the Law on Non-Governmental Organisations (Official Gazette of Montenegro 39/11, 37/17), the statute must regulate the following matters:

- **Name of the association.** As regards the name, bear in mind Article 13 of the Law, under which the name of a newly founded association must differ from the names of other associations entered in the relevant register. If two or more NGOs with the same name have submitted an application for entry in the register, the organisation that first submitted an orderly and complete application will be entered. The name of the association must be entered in the Montenegrin language. However, the name of the association in Montenegrin may contain foreign words if those words form the name of a foreign or international organisation of which it is a member, or words that are commonly used in the Montenegrin language, or for which there are no corresponding words in Montenegrin, or if they are Latin expressions. The Law also provides the possibility of entering the name of the association in a language in official use as well as in one or more foreign languages. The name of the association may contain the name of a natural person if that person has given consent, or, for a deceased person, if their heirs have given consent. A non-governmental association may also have an abbreviated name, which is determined by the statute.

- **Seat of the association.** Enter the name of the municipality in which the association has its seat.

- **Goals.** These provisions should clearly and concisely establish the basic goals and activities for which the association is founded, bearing in mind that the goals and action of the association may not be directed at the violent overthrow of the constitutional order and the violation of the territorial integrity of Montenegro, the violation of guaranteed human or minority rights, or the incitement of national, racial, religious and other hatred and intolerance.

- **Activities.** These provisions should define the specific activities the association will carry out in order to achieve its goals.

- **Internal organisation, bodies, their powers, composition, manner of election and recall, work and convening of sessions of governing bodies, quorum and manner of decision-making, duration of the term of members of governing bodies.** The Law prescribes that the governing bodies of an association are the Assembly and the person authorised for representation (director, president, general secretary and the like). The Law leaves open the possibility of giving a different name to the body that has the function of the assembly. Article 23 of the Law on NGOs provides that the statute may determine the manner in which members are represented in the assembly of the association through authorised proxies. This possibility may be particularly interesting and important for associations that have a large number of members and that are territorially dispersed, given that the same Article of the Law provides that a regular session of the assembly is held at least once a year. If a non-governmental association wishes to settle this question in a satisfactory normative way, we suggest adding to the statute a separate chapter "MANNER OF REPRESENTING MEMBERS IN THE ASSEMBLY OF THE ASSOCIATION THROUGH AUTHORISED PROXIES", which can be placed immediately after the chapter "QUORUM FOR WORK AND DECISION-MAKING" (i.e. after Article 17 of the Model Statute of an association prepared by CRNVO). **[Translator's note: CRNVO is the Centre for Development of Non-Governmental Organisations, the author of the statute template you received.]**

  This chapter can define what a proxy must contain, how it is obtained, whether proxies are arranged on a territorial basis or in relation to a certain number of assembly members or in some other way, etc. The Law also leaves open the possibility of providing for other bodies in the statute. Although they are not named in the Law, other bodies may be an Executive Board, a Management Board, a Supervisory Board and the like. Since the provisions on the bodies of the association are of essential importance for its functioning, the statute should regulate these matters very carefully, precisely and clearly (which bodies the association has, what their powers are, the types of acts the bodies adopt, the duties and responsibilities of each of those bodies, composition, election and dismissal, duration of the term, and the manner of decision-making of collective bodies: the quorum required for valid decision-making and the majority required for adopting decisions; if the assembly consists of representatives of members, the manner of election and duration of the term of those representatives should also be regulated). In addition, the term and the manner of recalling the assembly must be defined. It is usually defined as a four-year term, with recall by majority decision of the assembly.

- **Representation of the association.** Only a natural person with legal capacity who has permanent or temporary residence in the territory of Montenegro may be designated as the representative of the association. In practice, the power of representation is most often listed as one of the duties of an individual (single-person) body: president, executive director, general secretary and the like. The association chooses one title for the function. The Statute template gives the example of an Executive Director, but, as the Law itself prescribes, the association may appoint a president, general secretary and the like as the person authorised for representation. It is important that, once the title of this function is established in the statute, it is used with identical terminology throughout the entire document.

- **Procedure for amending and supplementing the statute and the manner of adopting other general acts of the association, if it adopts them.** The procedure for amending and supplementing the statute should be regulated clearly and precisely, so that there are no disputes about the regularity of particular amendments. The Law provides that the assembly of the association decides on amendments and supplements to the statute, but the statute should also regulate who may initiate amendments and supplements and whether a special quorum and/or a special majority of votes is required for the decision, and the like.

- **Public nature of work.** Provisions on how the public nature of the association's work is achieved usually provide for informing the public about the NGO's work through press releases, the association's website, publication of the annual financial report, and in other suitable ways.

- **Manner of financing, including provisions on economic or other income-generating activity, if the association carries it out.** When preparing the statute, particular attention should be paid to Articles 28 and 29 of the Law, i.e. that the association may acquire assets from membership fees, voluntary contributions, donations and gifts (in money or in kind), bequests, interest on deposits, rent, dividends and in other ways permitted by law. In addition, the association may carry out an income-generating economic activity in accordance with the law governing the classification of activities, under the conditions provided by that law (that the activity is related to its statutory goals, that it is provided for in the statute and that it is of smaller scale, i.e. that the activity is carried out to the extent needed to achieve the association's goals). The statute should clearly state which economic activities the association will engage in. An income-generating economic activity may be carried out only after the activity has been entered in the Central Register of Business Entities. As for the manner of using income earned from economic activity, the Law provides clear rules and restrictions, and these may be copied, or the statute may state that the relevant provisions of the Law on Non-Governmental Organisations apply directly to those matters. Provisions on carrying out an income-generating economic activity should be included in the statute only if the association carries out or intends to carry out such an activity. If the statute does not contain such provisions and the association later decides to obtain part of its funds in that way, appropriate amendments to the statute can be made (and the activity entered in the Central Register of Business Entities).

- **Disposal of the association's assets, including in the event of termination of work.** These provisions should regulate the disposal of the association's assets in relation to Article 30 of the Law, which provides that assets may be used only to achieve the goals for which the NGO was founded, as well as to reimburse justified costs incurred in achieving the NGO's goals, to pay salaries, remuneration and other earnings of employees on the basis of work, to give occasional awards, and other remuneration in accordance with the statute and project activities. Article 40 of the Law provides that, in the event of termination of the association's work, only another non-governmental organisation or a public institution with its seat in Montenegro that carries out activities in the areas of public interest under Article 32 paragraph 2 may be designated in the statute as the recipient of its assets. The Law also allows the statute not to designate a recipient of the assets, since Article 40 provides that in that case the association's assets pass to the local self-government in whose territory the organisation had its seat, and may be used to achieve the goals for which the organisation was founded.

- **Form and content of the seal.** These provisions should regulate what the NGO's seal looks like, bearing in mind that the name (and any abbreviated name) may be used only in the form in which they are entered in the register. Seals of associations usually contain the name and seat of the association and, possibly, a sign, logo or other symbol, if these are established by the statute. In any case, the NGO's seal must be made in accordance with the provision of the statute that establishes its appearance and content.

- **Termination of the association's work.** Article 38 of the Law provides the conditions for termination of work and deletion of the NGO from the register. As for decisions on amending the statute, for decisions on this matter it can be provided who is authorised to submit the proposal, whether a special quorum and/or a special majority of votes is required, and the like.

- **Conditions for acquiring and losing membership, rights and obligations of members, and the manner of keeping the register of members.** These provisions should regulate how one becomes a member of the association, what the rights and obligations of members are, how membership ends, who is entrusted with keeping the register of members, and in particular what the grounds for loss of membership are, which body decides on it and what the procedure for expulsion is (for example, whether the member has the right to an objection or appeal, and the like).

- The provision on the statute of non-governmental organisations provides that the statute may also regulate **other matters of importance for the action and work of the non-governmental organisation**. Which matters those are depends on the association itself, its goals and the planned way of achieving those goals.
