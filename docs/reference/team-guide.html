Why I started this

I live here. That is the whole idea.

Tivat is small enough that when a family is in trouble, you know them, or you know someone who does. Last December a group of us decided to stop feeling bad about it and organise something instead. We ran a marathon and a pub crawl, we asked our friends and our local businesses for help, and we gave away every single cent we raised.

It worked better than we expected, and it taught us the thing we didn't know: our community will show up. What was missing wasn't generosity. It was structure.

So this year we're building the structure.

BROKE

No written playbook, no list of who did what. If two of us had been ill, the event would not have happened.

Cash in pockets, promises by text, no receipts trail. Nothing went wrong — but nothing was stopping it from going wrong either.

Step one, and it's boring

How we become a real organisation.

We're registering as a non-governmental association (nevladino udruženje) — not a foundation. A foundation needs one founder and gives tighter control; an association has members, which is exactly what we are. It's also the structure grant funders take seriously.

01

Three founders

Only one needs residence or a business in Montenegro. Foreigners can found it. So can anyone 14+ with guardian consent.

02

Statute drafted

By a lawyer, €500–1,500. Cheapest insurance we'll ever buy — the clauses are painful to retrofit.

03

File with the Ministry

Ministry of Public Administration. Decision within 10 days of a complete application.

04

Seal → MONSTAT → PIB → bank

In that order. The bank account is the bottleneck for everything else, including taking cards.

The statute must cover fundraising, sport, culture, youth, social protection and environment — broadly enough that we're eligible for the widest range of public calls. It must also authorise chapters, and give an independent committee power over who receives money.

And the rules we're going to follow

The legal constraint, stated plainly.

Article 30 of the NGO Law says our assets may not be distributed between founders, members, board or employees. That prohibits distributing profit — not paying a market salary for work actually done. But because founders and staff sit in the same clause, the difference has to be obvious and documented.

01

Contracts, not transfers

Written contract, job description, documented hours, payroll with taxes paid. No exceptions, not even for founders.

02

The payer isn't the payee

Anyone taking a salary can't be in the board majority that sets it. Independent members are recruited before the first hire.

03

Bands published

Salary ranges in the annual report, and the benchmark recorded in the minutes.

04

No relatives on payroll

In the early years, full stop. Even where legal, it's the first thing a critic reaches for.

05

A written opinion first

From a Montenegrin accountant, before the first payroll. A €300 conversation that prevents an existential one.

Read this one twice

We can't use the tools everyone else uses.

Most people planning a fundraising platform in Montenegro discover this after building it. It is the single hardest constraint we face, and it shapes every technology decision.

CONSTRAINT

Stripe won't serve us

Stripe requires the business to be registered in a supported country. Montenegro isn't one. And Raisely, Funraise, Donorbox, Zeffy, GoFundMe Pro and Fundraise Up are all built on Stripe — we cannot simply sign up for any of them.

CONSTRAINT

PayPal can't receive here

PayPal has been in Montenegro for years, but only for sending. Receiving isn't available. Don't design anything around it.

OPPORTUNITY

But we joined SEPA

All Montenegrin banks were registered in the SEPA Credit Transfer scheme by June 2025, operational from 5 October 2025, with 24/7 instant payments arriving via TIPS Clone. A euro transfer from the EU is now as fast and cheap as a domestic one — and almost nobody here is exploiting it yet.

What this means practically: we build rather than buy, our card processing goes through a local provider, and our cheapest rail is the one nobody else is using.

The Monri account depends on our entity, PIB and bank account, and that timeline isn't fully in our control. The SEPA rail needs no third party. Building it first means we can accept money in December even if the card account clears late.

Cash has to be logged in the platform

A fundraiser who collects €120 at the office must be able to record it against their page. Otherwise our leaderboard lies — and a leaderboard that lies stops motivating anyone.

A SECOND PRIZE

Other NGOs could use it

Once it works for us, this is infrastructure other Montenegrin organisations need and can't buy either. That's a plausible future revenue line and a strong grant application.

Our answer to charity scepticism

We publish the ledger. Both directions.

In a country our size, reputation is the only asset that matters and the one thing we can't buy back. So we remove the argument entirely.

Every euro in

Date, amount, the donor's chosen display name or "Anonymous", which fundraiser it belongs to, which town. Generated from the database — not typed into a PDF once a year.

Every euro out

Date, amount, what it was for, and a link to the proof: receipt, invoice, signed handover, committee decision.

Append-only

Nothing is editable once published. Corrections appear as new, dated, visible rows. A ledger that can be quietly rewritten isn't a ledger.

Privacy still protected

"A family in Tivat, medical costs, €1,200" is enough for the public. Names and paperwork stay with our auditor, not the internet.

This also protects us. Cash counted by two people, same-day deposits, receipts for everything. Not because we distrust each other — because the person who handles money should never be the only person who can vouch for it.

All Stripe-dependent. Montenegro is not a supported merchant country. The door is closed, not expensive.

Fast, but peer-to-peer pages and the transparency ledger will fight us the whole way.

The decisive argument: whatever we bought, we'd still have to build the Monri integration and the SEPA matching ourselves. Once we're doing that, the remaining gap to a full build is small — and we end up owning the platform.

What we owe them back

Nobody needs another logo placement.

What a sponsor actually wants is a story their leadership can tell, photographs with their own people in them, something for their staff to do, and proof of what happened. So we build all four into every package — and deliver them without being chased.

A report that's theirs

Photos featuring their team, participation numbers, media coverage, funds raised, and exactly where the money went. Sent within 30 days, unprompted.

Their staff on a team page

Every package includes an employee team slot. Eighteen of their people fundraising beats one banner.

An invitation to the handover

They meet the people their money reached. Nothing we write is worth more than that.

Renewal becomes automatic

Deliver these four without being asked and next year's conversation is a formality. This is the cheapest growth available to us.

Someone needs to own sponsor delivery as a job. If it's nobody's job it silently becomes nobody's job.

What could go wrong, and what we've done about it

The things I'd rather we discussed now.

SEVERE

A water safety incident

No swim without qualified lifeguards, a safety boat, a briefing, a capacity limit and signed waivers — run with clubs who do this professionally. One incident ends the organisation.

SEVERE

Child safeguarding

Written policy, briefed volunteers, no unsupervised adult–child contact, parental photo consent, a named lead at every event. Non-negotiable.

HIGH

Salaries seen as self-enrichment

Independent board majority on pay, published bands, no relatives, a written legal opinion first. Slides 12 and 13 exist for this reason.

HIGH

Depending on one person

Chapter in a Box, documented playbooks, and a paid director whose job is making the founder replaceable.

HIGH

December arrives too fast

Better a beautiful 250-person event than a chaotic 600-person one. We set the cap low and sell out.

MEDIUM

VAT and tax surprises

Registration is required above €30,000 turnover. Donations aren't turnover — but entry fees, merchandise and sponsorship generally are. We model this with an accountant before selling 1,000 entries.

MEDIUM

Volunteer burnout

February's Impact Day, the thank-yous, and never asking the same twelve people for everything.

MEDIUM

Grant dependency

Capped at 30% of revenue. We decline funding that would turn us into a different organisation.

01

Is the December marathon ours or someone else's?

Sponsoring an existing event borrows their crowd, permits and timing. Organising from scratch in four months is a different plan entirely. This changes everything downstream.

02

Where does the €4–7k working capital come from?

Suits, medals, permits and insurance are paid before any entry fee arrives. Founder loan, early sponsor, or a smaller first event?

03

Who is the first beneficiary?

A name and a story, not a category. Nothing in the marketing works until this exists.

04

Which three of us are the founders?

Named on the registration. One needs residence here. This has legal weight and shouldn't be decided by who replies fastest.

05

Who fills the board and committee seats?

We need an accountant, a lawyer, a community voice, an event professional — and a committee majority who don't work for us.

06

Which three events in 2026?

Santa Run is certain. Crawl and Plunge are the obvious pair, but if capacity is thinner than we think, two done well beats three done badly.

What I'm asking of you

Read this, argue with it, then pick something and say it in writing.

Last December proved our community will show up. What we didn't have was a structure worth showing up to — no playbook, no way to take a card, no second event to invite anyone to, and everything living in a couple of people's heads.

This document is that structure. Most of it will be wrong somewhere, and the fastest way to find out is for the eight or ten of us who care most to go through it properly and disagree in the right places.
